Where do the 12 volts go?

Kirchhoff’s voltage law: the two voltage drops add to the battery voltage.

V₁ + V₂ = 12 V
Voltage budgetRemaining: 0.00 V
R₁ drop: 4.00 VR₂ drop: 8.00 V
4.00 V + 8.00 V = 12.00 V

All calculations use full precision; the displayed drops use complementary rounding to 0.01 V so the visible budget always adds to exactly 12.00 V.

1 · Series rule

Resistance adds: Rtotal = R₁ + R₂.

2 · Ohm’s law

The same current flows through both: I = 12 V / Rtotal.

3 · Voltage drops

V₁ = I × R₁ and V₂ = I × R₂; their sum is 12 V.

Physical assumptions: One closed loop, ideal 12 V DC source, ideal ohmic resistors, negligible wire resistance, and no internal battery resistance. The animation shows conventional current from positive to negative through the external circuit; real electron drift is in the opposite direction. Actual components have tolerances and may heat up; this is an ideal instructional model.
